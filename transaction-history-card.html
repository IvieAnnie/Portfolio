01 — The Constraint

The upgrade wasn't optional.

A security mandate meant the legacy Patient Transaction History had to be rebuilt on newer technology.

The easy path was parity. Instead, I used the migration to identify which parts of the existing workflow were worth preserving — and which friction we shouldn't carry forward.

Not just one screen: we pulled information from multiple legacy pop-ups to improve the workflow.

02 — Research

Find the friction before rebuilding it.

I conducted and synthesized research across five client sessions and three internal expert sessions, then compared those findings with existing customer feedback.

Four patterns shaped the redesign.

1

The workflow consisted of multiple screens

Billers had to navigate between several screens — like Charge Detail, Write-off Detail, and Edit Visit — to get the information they needed.

2

There wasn't one right view

Different roles needed different information from the same financial history. The legacy experience gave everyone the same fixed columns.

3

Review and action were separated

Transaction History was primarily for understanding what had happened. Actions like payment entry and write-offs lived elsewhere, adding navigation to workflows that often involved both.

4

We can now see charges grouped by visit

Billing experts followed the financial story of a visit — its charges, claims, payments, adjustments, and balance — rather than treating every transaction as an unrelated row.

“I don't work charges, I work visits.”

Research and existing feedback were synthesized into recurring workflow patterns rather than treated as individual feature requests.

03 — Design

Modernize the technology. Improve the workflow.

The new card keeps financial history at the center while bringing more of the work users do around it into the same experience.

Make the view yours.

Users can reorder, hide, show, and pin columns instead of working around a fixed layout. Preferred Views preserve the configuration that matters to each user's workflow.

Column customization and Preferred Views.

Keep the financial story together.

Visit-level rows expand into their underlying financial activity, while details and actions open alongside the history instead of replacing it.

Expanded visit with details and actions in a flyout.
1

Act in context

Payment entry, write-offs, collections, and other actions are accessible from the history workflow.

2

Flexible setup

Show and hide data, move columns, customized to the workflow.

3

Remember the setup

Saved views preserve columns, filters, and sorting for different workflows.

4

Start with useful data

The legacy “show all → reload” routine becomes the default rather than something users repeat every time they open the card.

04 — Measurement

Define success before beta.

Before rolling the redesign out broadly, we needed a clear way to know whether it was actually working.

Instead, I worked with Product to establish the signals we'd use to determine whether the redesign actually improved the workflow.

→

Back to Legacy

Are users abandoning the new experience?

→

Flyout usage

Are users completing more work without navigating away?

→

Preferred View saves

Are different roles adapting the card to their workflow?

→

Time on task

Is common financial work becoming faster?

The same early work also surfaced unresolved issues — including payment fragmentation and balance discrepancies between the new and legacy views — that needed attention before broader adoption.

Pendo usage across roles gave us the baseline. Success criteria were defined before beta so we could compare behavior against the legacy experience, rather than declare the redesign successful because it shipped.
