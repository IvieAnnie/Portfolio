One pattern for AI across the product

Seven AI features, built by different teams at the same time. I kept them on one pattern: at every step, the AI either tells people what it did or leaves a record they can check.

01 — The Problem

AI was arriving from several teams at once

Collections, clinical, and billing teams were all adding AI at the same time. Two teams had already started designing their own AI summary. Billers and clinicians are careful for good reason: a wrong posting means a wrong patient balance, and a wrong chart suggestion means a wrong record.

If every AI feature explained itself differently, users would have to rebuild their trust on every screen.

03 — One Pattern

How every AI feature looks and behaves

I pulled the teams together on one design and mentored the designer on my team who built the clinical features on it. Every feature offers feedback and an audit trail at every step. The wording fits the domain, so clinicians review for “clinical accuracy,” but the parts stay the same.

Every summary has the same five parts: 1the AI mark in its name, 2a request to review, 3thumbs up and down, 4an update button, so it runs only when asked, and 5the time it was generated. Because feedback works the same everywhere, practices that subscribe can tell us what’s working wherever they see AI.

The feedback loop

  1. The AI shows its workAn AI label, its sources or reasoning, and its status.
  2. People check itReview, accept or reject, or override, without leaving the task.
  3. They respond in one clickThumbs up and down on every summary and answer. Accepts, rejects, and overrides are logged.
  4. It comes back to usThe product team reads one set of signals across all seven features and decides what to fix.

04 — The Rules

Four rules every AI feature follows

1

People choose how much the AI does

Each practice chooses, per payer, whether payment posting only recommends or posts automatically. Agents are labeled read-only or write.

2

Suggestions stay suggestions

Clinical suggestions are accepted or rejected one at a time. Anything the AI posts can be overridden, and overrides are logged.

3

Every step can be checked

The AI either shows what it did, like the status on every payment line, or leaves a record people can check later, like each agent’s run history and the log of overrides. Assistant answers link to their sources.

4

No guessing, no side effects

With too little to go on, the AI says so instead of filling space, the same rule I gave our research model. When it fails, the work is left exactly as it was.

05 — Results

What we’ve seen so far

The product team’s early assessment of the collections summary: a 75% reduction in manual note review time per claim, and follow-ups that no longer depend on which biller works the account. Next, we’re watching the thumbs feedback, how often billers override the AI, and whether practices move from “recommend only” to automatic posting.

What I learned

Consistency had to be someone’s job. Each team was making reasonable choices for its own screen, and nothing would have pulled them together without someone deciding that AI should look and behave one way across the product.
