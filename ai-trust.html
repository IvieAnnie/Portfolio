01 — The Problem

Billing teams were wary of AI handling their work

Insurance payment posting and collections follow-up are some of the most repetitive work in a medical practice. They’re also some of the most consequential. A wrong posting means a wrong patient balance, a confused patient on the phone, and someone on the billing team cleaning it up.

So the people doing this work are careful, and they’re skeptical of anything that promises to “just handle it.” An AI that posts payments or summarizes a visit’s history is asking them to hand over something they’re responsible for.

The question wasn’t whether the AI could do the work. It was what the people accountable for the work would need to see before they’d let it.

02 — One Summary

Unifying two teams’ AI summary designs

Two product teams had each started designing an AI summary for different screens. I pulled them together so the summary looks and behaves the same everywhere: the same placement, labels, states, and feedback.

That matters more for AI than for most features. If the AI explains itself one way in collections and another way somewhere else, users have to rebuild their trust each time. With one pattern, they learn it once.

03 — Principles

Seven principles for trustworthy AI features

1

People choose how much the AI does

The practice decides, per payer, whether the AI only recommends or posts automatically. The on/off switch says in plain words what will happen: “Matching ERAs post automatically.”

2

Every line shows its status

Auto-posted with a confidence score, posting, pending, or an exception with the reason. Nothing happens off-screen.

3

Suggestions look like suggestions

When the AI isn’t sure, it says so, “AI suggestion — review before acting,” and shows the candidate visits so a biller can choose.

4

Anything the AI posts can be overridden

A biller can override any AI posting, and overrides and rule changes are logged. Control never moves out of reach.

5

Fail without side effects

If the AI service doesn’t respond, the work is left exactly as it was: “The claim line is unchanged — you can retry or work it manually.”

6

Say so when there isn’t enough to go on

If there isn’t enough new collections activity, the summary doesn’t stretch to fill the space. It says “Not enough new collections activity to summarize yet.” It’s the same rule I gave our research model: an honest gap beats a confident guess.

7

Ask for feedback where the output is

Every summary is labeled as AI-generated, asks the user to review it, shows when it was generated, and has thumbs up and down right beside it.

04 — The Screens

The four states of the AI collections summary

The collections summary has four states, and each one tells the user exactly where they stand. Summaries only run when someone asks for one.

AI status on every payment line

In the payment posting design, every line carries its own AI status. Billers can see at a glance what posted, what’s in progress, and what needs them, along with the reason.
